*The Pragmatic Programmer: Your Journey to Mastery* (20th Anniversary Edition, Addison-Wesley 2020) is David Thomas & Andrew Hunt's revised classic — the book that coined DRY, orthogonality, tracer bullets, and stone soup. Where *A Philosophy of Software Design* goes deep on one idea (complexity), this one covers the whole craft: how to hold yourself accountable, how to keep knowledge fresh, how to build software that bends instead of breaking.

It's organized into 9 chapters and 53 numbered **Topics**, with **100 one-line Tips** distilled from them — the notes here follow the chapter structure and collect the Tips worth remembering in each.

Nice pairing with APOSD: "software entropy" and broken windows ≈ complexity is incremental; orthogonality and decoupling ≈ deep modules and information hiding. The two books argue the same fight from different altitudes.

## How these notes work

One note per chapter, topic-by-topic, ending with an empty **"My takeaways"** section. Each note also collects the numbered Tips worth remembering from that chapter.
